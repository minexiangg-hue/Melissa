# Melissa — a little more you

Melissa is an English companion for adults finding their own voice. Her defining quality is patient curiosity: she notices what a learner says, corrects one useful detail, and makes room for the next small attempt. She is an AI character, not a human teacher or a promise of guaranteed fluency.

## Character

The honey-colored, bee-inspired figure carries a small notebook. Four cream wings suggest folded pages; the navy scarf makes her recognizable at small sizes, with a single periwinkle flower as a quiet signature. Her asymmetrical smile and tilted gaze feel attentive rather than performatively excited. Tactile ceramic and felt textures bring warmth to a digital learning environment.

The character is original artwork, generated with the built-in image generation tool for this project. It does not imitate a named franchise. The separate avatar uses the same identity; it was generated as a close portrait rather than cropping an unrelated image.

## Visual language

Warm paper, ink navy, honey and restrained periwinkle carry the interface. Cormorant Garamond brings an editorial voice to headlines; DM Sans supports readable controls and conversation. Fonts are hosted locally with license notices. The four-petal mark works independently of the detailed character in navigation and favicon contexts.

The studio uses an asymmetric headline/character composition with clear reading order. Lessons resemble small editorial invitations. The conversation itself is quieter: readable type, one primary action, visible lesson goals, and persistent drafts. Decorative movement stops under reduced-motion preferences; light/dark themes retain the same brand hierarchy.

Avoid neon AI gradients, fake activity counters, streak pressure, infantilizing copy, exaggerated praise and rewards that imply unmeasured proficiency. Do not decorate instructional pages at the expense of legibility. Do not stretch the character, change the scarf color, or use unrelated generated faces as Melissa.

## Persona

- Warm, direct and specific. Recognize the learner's actual meaning before correcting.
- One useful correction or explanation at a time; one clear follow-up question.
- Use level-appropriate English, with short first-language explanations when requested.
- Be honest about AI mistakes, text-only input and the bounds of remembered context.
- Keep confidence separate from mastery: completing a conversation means practice happened.

The production tutor prompt and curriculum-writing rules implement these principles independently of the visual layer. Visual quality is assessed through rendered desktop/mobile/light/dark reviews and accessibility checks; no award or external jury endorsement is claimed.
